# Stage 01 — Exception of Distance

## Purpose

Design and deliver the Relation mastery ordeal that proves the player can
author adjacency responsibly. Its sole progression output is the
**Exception of Distance**.

## Player Promise

The player does not receive the Exception for crafting an expensive object or
using Relation repeatedly. They receive it after creating an impossible
connection, allowing another subject to depend on it, and closing it without
stranding, duplicating, or erasing anything.

## Required Design

- Entry requires the Manuscript of Relation at Authorship.
- The ordeal takes place in a bounded Architectonic location whose blocks and
  entities can be restored.
- The challenge must contain two places that ordinary movement cannot join.
- The player must establish a temporary adjacency, permit a non-player subject
  to cross it, and close the authored boundary safely.
- Success must demonstrate both intervention and Closure.
- Failure restores the location and permits another attempt without consuming
  unique materials.
- Multiplayer may assist with combat or observation, but only the owner whose
  Manuscript began the ordeal receives the Exception.

## Progression Contract

The Exception is a unique, owner-bound proof. It must survive death, reject
duplication, and be recoverable if its physical representation is lost. The
Broker exchange consumes the player's proof state and its item representation
atomically.

Granting it should produce a concise line:

> Distance is now a relation, not a prohibition.

## Design Gate

Before implementation, a focused brainstorming pass must choose the exact
location, crossing subject, authored-boundary controls, and failure events.
That design must preserve the proof above and must not reduce the ordeal to a
timed parkour course or inventory check.

## Verification

- Ineligible players cannot begin or receive the Exception.
- Safe Closure, not merely opening the path, triggers completion.
- Subject death, player death, logout, or chunk unload restores the ordeal.
- Repeating the ordeal cannot produce a second usable proof.
- Two players completing separate ordeals cannot affect each other's state.
